---
title: Warm-up and sending pace
description: Why a number starts slowly, how fast it may go, and what changes the speed.
---

A new or quiet WhatsApp number that suddenly sends many messages looks like spam, and WhatsApp can ban it for good. So the CRM makes every number **warm up** and paces every message. This page explains what you will see.

## The daily limit while warming up

| Day | Most messages that day |
|---|---|
| 1 | 20 |
| 2 | 36 |
| 3 | 65 |
| 4 | 117 |
| 5 | 210 |
| 6 | 378 |
| 7 | 680 |
| 8 and after | your normal daily limit (1,500 unless changed) |

A number that has been idle for **72 hours** warms up again. A number that has **never received a reply** is held to **20 a day** until its first reply arrives, because sending without ever being answered is a classic ban pattern.

:::note
Many other tools recommend starting even slower, for example a handful a day for a new number. These steps are a ceiling, not a target. For a number carrying cold leads, going slower is safer.
:::

## The pause before each message

Each message waits a random moment, usually **1.5 to 4 seconds**, so there is never a fixed rhythm. That pause is then multiplied:

| Who you are messaging | Slowdown |
|---|---|
| Someone who has replied to you | ×1 (fastest) |
| Someone you messaged before who has not replied | ×1.8 |
| A stranger | ×2.5 |

Early morning and late evening stretch it further, and a number whose delivery is poor is slowed up to four times. On top of that comes a short **"typing…"** moment, longer for longer messages. Together these are why a cold-lead campaign sends roughly one message every ten seconds.

## After a reconnect

For ten minutes after a number reconnects it sends at most **3 a minute**, so a backlog does not rush out at once.

## What speeds a number up

Time and replies. The first reply lifts the 20-a-day hold. People who reply move from "stranger" to "known", which removes the 2.5× slowdown. Good delivery keeps the health score green. Nothing in the app can switch these protections off for a real number, and an admin override that exists for throwaway test numbers is clearly marked as permanent-ban territory.

## What you see in the app

- The **Linked numbers** page shows each number's health, risk score and any cool-down.
- A campaign's **dry run** shows how long it will take ("about 3 days to finish at 40/day").
- A message that is being held says why, in the inbox.
